## 1. EXAM OVERVIEW & STRATEGY

### Exam Format
- **100 multiple-choice questions**, each with ONE best answer
- **2 hours 30 minutes** (150 minutes)
- **Computer Adaptive Testing (CAT)**: The test adjusts difficulty based on your answers. When you answer correctly, the next question gets harder. When you answer incorrectly, the next question gets easier.
- **Scaled score range**: 0–999
- **Minimum passing score**: 400
- There is NO set number of questions you must answer correctly — the CAT algorithm determines pass/fail based on your ability measure relative to the pass point.

### Content Breakdown

| Content Area | % of Exam | ~# of Questions |
|---|---|---|
| Blood Banking | 17–22% | 17–22 |
| Chemistry | 17–22% | 17–22 |
| Hematology | 17–22% | 17–22 |
| Microbiology | 17–22% | 17–22 |
| Urinalysis & Body Fluids | 5–10% | 5–10 |
| Immunology | 5–10% | 5–10 |
| Laboratory Operations | 5–10% | 5–10 |

### Strategy for Filipino MedTech Graduates

Your Philippine MedTech curriculum covers the same core sciences. The key differences you need to bridge:

1. **US-specific regulations**: CLIA, AABB, FDA, CAP — these are heavily tested. The Philippines uses DOH and PhilHealth standards; the ASCP tests US regulatory frameworks.
2. **Blood Banking terminology**: US uses "type and screen" / "type and crossmatch" terminology. Gel column technology (column agglutination test) is heavily tested.
3. **Antibody identification panels**: The ASCP loves panel interpretation questions. Master rule-out procedures.
4. **Microbiology identification algorithms**: Know MALDI-TOF MS, automated ID systems, and molecular methods. These may have been less emphasized in your Philippine training.
5. **Quality control / quality assurance**: Levey-Jennings charts, Westgard rules, delta checks — these are tested in Lab Operations AND across all departments.
6. **Reference ranges**: You must MEMORIZE the ASCP reference ranges (listed at the end of this guide). They will NOT be provided on the exam.

### Study Plan Recommendation

**If you have 8 weeks:**
- Weeks 1–2: Chemistry + Hematology (the two biggest, most complex areas)
- Weeks 3–4: Blood Banking + Microbiology (the other two big areas)
- Week 5: Urinalysis/Body Fluids + Immunology + Lab Operations
- Week 6: Full practice exams, identify weak areas
- Week 7: Targeted review of weak areas + clinical scenarios
- Week 8: Final review, light study, rest before exam

**If you have 4 weeks (accelerated):**
- Week 1: Chemistry + Hematology (heavy days, 4–6 hours)
- Week 2: Blood Banking + Microbiology
- Week 3: UA/BF + Immunology + Lab Ops + practice exams
- Week 4: Weak area review + final practice exam

## 2. BLOOD BANKING (17–22%)

Blood Banking is one of the four major content areas. Many Filipino MedTech graduates find this section challenging because the ASCP emphasizes antibody identification panels, transfusion reactions, and AABB regulations more heavily than Philippine board exams.

### 2.1 Blood Products

#### Donor Qualification
- **Age**: Typically 16–17+ (with parental consent) to no upper limit if healthy
- **Weight**: Minimum 110 lbs (50 kg) for standard whole blood donation
- **Hemoglobin/Hematocrit**: Hgb ≥ 12.5 g/dL or Hct ≥ 38%
- **Donation interval**: Whole blood — every 8 weeks (56 days); Double RBC — every 16 weeks; Platelets — every 48 hours (up to 24x/year)
- **Temperature**: ≤ 99.5°F (37.5°C)
- **Pulse**: 50–100 bpm
- **Blood pressure**: Systolic ≤ 180, Diastolic ≤ 100
- **Permanent deferrals**: History of hepatitis B/C after age 11, HIV positive, IV drug use, Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (CJD) risk, Ebola virus disease

#### Anticoagulants/Preservatives — CRITICAL TABLE

| Anticoagulant | Shelf Life (RBCs) | Key Component |
|---|---|---|
| ACD (Acid Citrate Dextrose) | 21 days | Citrate chelates calcium |
| CPD (Citrate Phosphate Dextrose) | 21 days | Phosphate maintains 2,3-DPG |
| CP2D (Citrate Phosphate Double Dextrose) | 21 days | Extra dextrose for platelets |
| CPDA-1 | 35 days | Adenine extends RBC viability |
| Additive solutions (AS-1, AS-3, AS-5, AS-7) | 42 days | Added AFTER plasma removal |

> **Memory trick**: "21-21-21-35-42" — ACD, CPD, CP2D all = 21 days. CPDA-1 = 35. Additive solutions = 42.

#### Blood Component Storage — MUST MEMORIZE

| Component | Storage Temp | Shelf Life | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whole Blood | 1–6°C | 21–35 days | Depends on anticoagulant |
| Packed RBCs | 1–6°C | 35–42 days | CPDA-1 or additive solutions |
| Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP) | ≤ -18°C | 1 year | Must freeze within 8 hours of collection |
| Frozen Plasma (FP) | ≤ -18°C | 1 year | Frozen within 24 hours |
| Cryoprecipitate | ≤ -18°C | 1 year | Contains Factor VIII, fibrinogen, vWF, Factor XIII, fibronectin |
| Platelets (random donor) | 20–24°C with agitation | 5 days | Must be on continuous agitation |
| Platelets (apheresis) | 20–24°C with agitation | 5 days | Equivalent to 6 random donor units |
| Frozen RBCs (glycerolized) | ≤ -65°C | 10 years | Once thawed/deglycerolized: 24 hours at 1–6°C |
| Granulocytes | 20–24°C (no agitation) | 24 hours | Irradiate before transfusion |
| Thawed FFP | 1–6°C | 24 hours (5 days if relabeled as "Thawed Plasma") | |

> **High-yield**: Once thawed, cryoprecipitate must be transfused within 6 hours if single unit, or 4 hours if pooled. Pooled platelets expire 4 hours after pooling.

#### Irradiated Components
- **Purpose**: Prevent transfusion-associated graft-versus-host disease (TA-GVHD)
- **Method**: Gamma irradiation (minimum 25 Gy to center of component)
- **Indications**: HPC/bone marrow transplant recipients, intrauterine transfusions, directed donations from blood relatives, severely immunocompromised patients (e.g., Hodgkin lymphoma), premature neonates
- **Effect on RBCs**: Increases potassium leak — shortens expiration to 28 days from irradiation date OR original expiration, whichever comes FIRST

### 2.2 Blood Group Systems

#### ABO System — THE FOUNDATION

| Blood Type | Antigens on RBCs | Antibodies in Plasma | Can Receive RBCs From | Can Donate RBCs To |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | A antigen | Anti-B | A, O | A, AB |
| B | B antigen | Anti-A | B, O | B, AB |
| AB | A and B antigens | Neither (universal plasma donor for FFP) | A, B, AB, O (universal recipient) | AB |
| O | Neither (H antigen only) | Anti-A and Anti-B | O only | A, B, AB, O (universal donor) |

**ABO Discrepancies** — The exam LOVES these:
- **Extra antibodies in serum (reverse grouping)**: cold autoantibodies, alloantibodies, rouleaux
- **Missing/weak antigens (forward grouping)**: subgroups of A (A1 vs A2), leukemia, excessive blood group substance
- **Missing antibodies**: age (neonates <4–6 months don't have antibodies yet, elderly may lose them), immunosuppression, hypogammaglobulinemia, bone marrow transplant with ABO-incompatible donor

**A subgroups**:
- **A1**: 80% of group A individuals. Reacts with anti-A AND anti-A1 lectin (Dolichos biflorus)
- **A2**: 20% of group A. Reacts with anti-A but NOT anti-A1 lectin. Has more H antigen on RBC surface. ~1–8% of A2 individuals make anti-A1 (clinically insignificant unless reactive at 37°C)

#### Rh System
- **Most immunogenic antigen after A and B**: D antigen
- **Five principal Rh antigens**: D, C, c, E, e
- Fisher-Race notation: DCe/dce = R1r (most common in Caucasians)
- **Weak D (formerly Du)**: Reduced expression of D antigen. Test with anti-D at AHG phase. If positive = weak D positive = Rh positive.
- **Partial D**: Missing some D epitopes. CAN make anti-D if exposed to "complete" D antigen. Should receive Rh-negative blood.
- **RhIG (RhoGAM)**: Given to Rh-negative mothers to prevent alloimmunization. One dose covers ~30 mL whole blood / 15 mL packed RBCs. Rosette test = screening for fetomaternal hemorrhage (FMH). Kleihauer-Betke = quantitative FMH.

#### Other Important Blood Group Systems

| System | Antigens | Antibodies | Clinical Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kell | K (K1), k (K2), Kpa, Kpb | Anti-K: clinically significant, causes severe HTR and HDFN | Treat with 2-ME or DTT (destroys Kell antigens) |
| Kidd | Jka, Jkb | Anti-Jka/Jkb: NOTORIOUS for delayed hemolytic transfusion reactions; shows dosage; antibody levels drop quickly (hard to detect) | "Kidd will KILL you — and you won't see it coming" |
| Duffy | Fya, Fyb | Anti-Fya clinically significant; Fy(a-b-) common in Black individuals = resistance to P. vivax malaria | |
| MNS | M, N, S, s | Anti-M usually cold, IgM, clinically insignificant (unless reactive at 37°C); Anti-S and Anti-s clinically significant | Enzyme-resistant (ficin/papain destroys M, N, S) |
| Lewis | Lea, Leb | IgM, cold-reactive, NOT clinically significant; Lewis antibodies DO NOT cause HDFN (Lewis antigens are NOT intrinsic to RBCs — adsorbed from plasma) | |
| P1PK | P1 | Anti-P1: IgM, cold, clinically insignificant | |
| Ii | I, i | Anti-I: cold autoantibody associated with Mycoplasma pneumoniae; Anti-i: associated with infectious mononucleosis (EBV) | |
| Lutheran | Lua, Lub | Generally clinically insignificant | |

> **Memory trick for enzyme treatment**:
> Enzymes (ficin, papain, trypsin) **ENHANCE**: Rh, Kidd, Lewis, P1, I
> Enzymes **DESTROY**: MNS (M, N, S, s), Duffy (Fya, Fyb)
> **Unaffected**: Kell, Lutheran
> Mnemonic: "**Enzymes Destroy My Fly Safely**" — M/N/S (MNS) and Fy (Duffy) are destroyed

### 2.3 Antibody Identification

This is one of the most heavily tested topics. You will likely get 2–4 questions requiring panel interpretation.

#### Rule-Out Method (Crossing Out)
1. Look at all panel cells that are NEGATIVE (0 or no reaction)
2. For each negative cell, cross out any antigen that is POSITIVE on that cell — that antigen is "ruled out" because if the patient had antibody against it, that cell would have reacted
3. Whatever antigens remain NOT crossed out are your suspects
4. Confirm by finding cells positive for the suspected antigen (should react) and cells negative for it (should not react)

#### Rule of Three
- To confirm a single antibody specificity: You need at least 3 antigen-positive cells that react AND 3 antigen-negative cells that do not react — this gives statistical significance (p ≤ 0.05)

#### Common Antibody Patterns on Panels

| Pattern | Think... |
|---|---|
| Reactions at IS (immediate spin) only, no 37°C or AHG | Cold antibody (IgM): Anti-M, Anti-N, Anti-P1, Anti-Lea, Anti-Leb, Anti-I |
| Reactions at AHG phase only | Warm IgG antibody: Anti-D, Anti-K, Anti-Fya, Anti-Jka, Anti-S |
| Dosage (stronger reactions with homozygous cells) | Kidd, Duffy, MNS, Rh (C, c, E, e) — NOT Kell |
| Reactions with ALL panel cells and autocontrol positive | Warm autoantibody (WAIHA) — need autoadsorption to find underlying alloantibodies |
| Reactions with ALL panel cells, autocontrol negative | Consider antibody to high-prevalence antigen OR multiple antibodies |

#### Direct Antiglobulin Test (DAT) vs. Indirect Antiglobulin Test (IAT)

| | DAT | IAT |
|---|---|---|
| Detects | In vivo sensitization (antibody already coating patient's RBCs) | In vitro sensitization (antibody in patient's serum that reacts with test RBCs) |
| Specimen | Patient's washed RBCs + AHG reagent | Patient's serum + reagent/donor RBCs → incubate → wash → add AHG |
| Clinical use | HDFN, AIHA, HTR, drug-induced hemolytic anemia | Antibody screen, crossmatch, antibody ID |

### 2.4 Transfusion Reactions — HIGH YIELD

| Reaction Type | Timing | Cause | Key Lab Findings | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Acute Hemolytic (AHTR)** | During or within 24 hrs | ABO incompatibility (usually clerical error) | Positive DAT, hemoglobinemia, hemoglobinuria, ↑bilirubin, ↓haptoglobin, + plasma hemoglobin | STOP transfusion, IV fluids, monitor renal function, send samples to blood bank |
| **Delayed Hemolytic (DHTR)** | 3–14 days post-transfusion | Anamnestic response (usually Kidd, Rh, Kell) | Positive DAT, ↓Hgb, ↑bilirubin, new antibody on panel | Usually self-limiting; future crossmatch-compatible units |
| **Febrile Non-Hemolytic (FNHTR)** | During or within 4 hrs | Recipient antibodies to donor WBC antigens OR cytokines in stored components | Fever (≥1°C rise), chills, NO hemolysis | Antipyretics; use leukoreduced products in future |
| **Allergic** | During transfusion | Recipient IgE to donor plasma proteins | Urticaria, hives, itching, NO fever | Antihistamines (diphenhydramine); can restart transfusion after symptoms resolve |
| **Anaphylactic** | After small volume | IgA-deficient patient with anti-IgA | Hypotension, bronchospasm, NO fever, NO urticaria | STOP transfusion, epinephrine; use washed or IgA-deficient donor products |
| **TRALI** | Within 6 hours | Donor antibodies to recipient WBC antigens (HLA/HNA antibodies) | Bilateral pulmonary infiltrates, non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema, hypoxia | Supportive care; respiratory support. Do NOT give diuretics (not fluid overload) |
| **TACO** | During or within 6 hrs | Volume overload | Pulmonary edema, hypertension, ↑BNP | Diuretics, slow transfusion rate |
| **TA-GVHD** | 4–30 days | Donor T-cells engraft and attack recipient tissues | Pancytopenia, rash, liver dysfunction, diarrhea | Almost always fatal (>90% mortality). Prevention: IRRADIATE components |
| **Bacterial Contamination** | During or shortly after | Bacteria in component (especially platelets stored at 20–24°C) | High fever, rigors, hypotension, DIC | Broad-spectrum antibiotics, Gram stain/culture of bag and patient blood |

> **Exam pearl**: TRALI vs TACO — both present with respiratory distress. TRALI = bilateral infiltrates, normal/low BP, normal BNP, fever possible. TACO = elevated BP, elevated BNP, no fever. TRALI: do NOT give diuretics. TACO: give diuretics.

### 2.5 Hemolytic Disease of the Fetus and Newborn (HDFN)

- **Cause**: Maternal IgG antibodies cross the placenta and destroy fetal RBCs
- **Most common cause of severe HDFN**: Anti-D (Rh)
- **ABO HDFN**: Usually mild, affects Group A or B babies of Group O mothers. Mom's naturally occurring anti-A,B is IgG (Group O individuals have IgG anti-A,B, unlike Group A or B who have IgM anti-B or anti-A)
- **Lab findings in affected neonate**: Positive DAT, ↑bilirubin (unconjugated/indirect), ↑reticulocytes, spherocytes on smear (in ABO HDFN), anemia
- **RhIG (RhoGAM)**: Given at 28 weeks gestation and within 72 hours postpartum to Rh-negative mothers with Rh-positive babies
- **Rosette test**: Qualitative screen for fetal-maternal hemorrhage (FMH). If positive → quantitative Kleihauer-Betke (acid elution) test
- **Kleihauer-Betke**: Fetal hemoglobin (HbF) resists acid elution → fetal cells stain pink/red, maternal cells appear as ghosts

**Calculating RhIG dose**:
1. KB % fetal cells × maternal blood volume (5000 mL) = volume of fetal whole blood in maternal circulation
2. Divide by 30 mL = number of vials
3. If remainder exists, round UP and add 1 vial

### Blood Banking Clinical Scenarios

---

**Scenario BB-1**: A 67-year-old male with a history of colon cancer surgery 3 years ago (received 2 units of RBCs at that time) is now scheduled for hip replacement. His type and screen shows: Forward grouping = Group A; Rh positive. Antibody screen is positive at AHG phase only. Panel shows reactions with cells that are Jka-positive and no reactions with Jka-negative cells. Autocontrol is negative.

**Q: What is the most likely antibody?**
A) Anti-Jka
B) Anti-Jkb
C) Anti-K
D) Anti-Lea

**Answer: A) Anti-Jka**
Rationale: Jka antibodies are IgG (reactive at AHG phase), show dosage, and are notorious for dropping to undetectable levels and then showing anamnestic response upon re-exposure. This patient was likely sensitized during transfusion 3 years ago. Kidd antibodies are the #1 cause of delayed hemolytic transfusion reactions. Anti-Lea is IgM (reacts at IS, not AHG). Anti-K doesn't show dosage.

---

**Scenario BB-2**: A 30-year-old woman, G2P1, presents at 28 weeks gestation. She is Group O, Rh-negative. Her antibody screen is negative. Her husband is Rh-positive.

**Q: What is the appropriate next step?**
A) No intervention needed since the antibody screen is negative
B) Administer one dose of RhIG (300 μg) now at 28 weeks
C) Wait until delivery to determine if RhIG is needed
D) Perform a Kleihauer-Betke test now

**Answer: B) Administer one dose of RhIG at 28 weeks**
Rationale: Standard of care is to give RhIG prophylactically at 28 weeks gestation to all Rh-negative mothers with Rh-positive (or unknown) fetuses, regardless of antibody screen results. A second dose is given within 72 hours postpartum if the baby is confirmed Rh-positive.

---

**Scenario BB-3**: You are performing a crossmatch. The patient is Group A, Rh-positive. You select a unit of Group A, Rh-positive packed RBCs. The immediate spin crossmatch is compatible (no agglutination). The AHG phase crossmatch shows 2+ agglutination. The patient's antibody screen is positive. Panel identifies anti-K.

**Q: What should you do?**
A) Issue the unit — the immediate spin was compatible
B) Antigen-type the donor unit for K antigen
C) Switch to electronic crossmatch
D) Perform an auto-adsorption

**Answer: B) Antigen-type the donor unit for K antigen**
Rationale: The patient has anti-K. The crossmatch is incompatible at AHG, meaning the donor unit is likely K-positive. You must find K-negative units. Antigen-type the unit to confirm, then select K-negative, crossmatch-compatible units.

---

**Scenario BB-4**: A 45-year-old patient arrives in the ER with massive hemorrhage following a motor vehicle accident. The patient's blood type is unknown. Which component should be released for immediate emergency transfusion?

**Q: What is the best choice?**
A) Group A, Rh-negative packed RBCs
B) Group O, Rh-negative packed RBCs
C) Group AB, Rh-positive packed RBCs
D) Group O, Rh-positive packed RBCs

**Answer: B) Group O, Rh-negative packed RBCs**
Rationale: In emergency situations when the patient's blood type is unknown, Group O (universal donor) RBCs are given. Rh-negative is preferred, especially for females of childbearing potential, to prevent Rh sensitization. For males or post-menopausal females, Rh-positive may be acceptable if Rh-negative is in short supply.

## 3. CHEMISTRY (17–22%)

Clinical Chemistry is one of the largest and most content-dense sections. Focus heavily on disease state correlations and understanding what each analyte means clinically.

### 3.1 Carbohydrates

#### Glucose Metabolism
- **Fasting glucose reference range**: 74–100 mg/dL (memorize this)
- **Diabetes diagnosis criteria (ADA)**:
  - Fasting plasma glucose ≥ 126 mg/dL (on two occasions)
  - 2-hour OGTT ≥ 200 mg/dL
  - Random glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL with symptoms
  - HbA1c ≥ 6.5%
- **Prediabetes**: Fasting glucose 100–125 mg/dL, HbA1c 5.7–6.4%
- **HbA1c reference**: < 5.7% (memorize)
- HbA1c reflects average glucose over 2–3 months (RBC lifespan = ~120 days)
- **Falsely decreased HbA1c**: hemolytic anemia, sickle cell disease, chronic blood loss (anything that shortens RBC lifespan)
- **Falsely increased HbA1c**: iron deficiency anemia (RBCs live longer), splenectomy

#### Specimen Considerations
- **Sodium fluoride** (gray top tube): inhibits glycolysis (enolase inhibitor) — prevents glucose consumption by cells
- Without preservative, glucose decreases ~5–7% per hour at room temperature
- **Serum glucose is lower than whole blood glucose** because RBCs consume glucose

#### Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT)
- 75g glucose load for non-pregnant adults
- Gestational diabetes screening: 50g 1-hour screen → if abnormal, 100g 3-hour diagnostic OGTT

#### Hypoglycemia
- Insulinoma: ↑insulin, ↑C-peptide, ↓glucose
- Exogenous insulin administration: ↑insulin, ↓C-peptide, ↓glucose (C-peptide distinguishes exogenous from endogenous insulin)

### 3.2 Lipids and Lipoproteins

#### Lipoprotein Classes — Ordered by Density (low → high)

| Lipoprotein | Primary Lipid Carried | Apolipoproteins | Clinical Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chylomicrons | Dietary triglycerides | Apo B-48, C-II, E | "Creamy" layer on top of refrigerated serum = chylomicrons |
| VLDL | Endogenous triglycerides | Apo B-100, C-II, E | Precursor to LDL |
| IDL | Intermediate | Apo B-100, E | Transitional between VLDL and LDL |
| LDL | Cholesterol | Apo B-100 | "Bad" cholesterol — primary target for therapy |
| HDL | Cholesterol (reverse transport) | Apo A-I, A-II | "Good" cholesterol — protective |

#### Friedewald Equation — MUST KNOW
**LDL = Total Cholesterol – HDL – (Triglycerides / 5)**

- Only valid when Triglycerides < 400 mg/dL
- Patient must be fasting (12–14 hours)
- If TG ≥ 400, must use direct LDL measurement

#### Non-HDL Cholesterol
**Non-HDL = Total Cholesterol – HDL**
- Includes all atherogenic lipoproteins (LDL + VLDL + IDL + Lp(a))

### 3.3 Liver Function / Heme Derivatives

#### Bilirubin Metabolism
1. Old RBCs destroyed → Hemoglobin released → Heme → Biliverdin → **Unconjugated (indirect) bilirubin**
2. Unconjugated bilirubin binds albumin → transported to liver
3. Liver conjugates with glucuronic acid (by UDP-glucuronyltransferase) → **Conjugated (direct) bilirubin**
4. Conjugated bilirubin → bile → intestines → urobilinogen → stercobilin (feces) or reabsorbed and excreted in urine

#### Bilirubin in Jaundice

| Type | Unconjugated (Indirect) | Conjugated (Direct) | Urine Bilirubin | Urine Urobilinogen | Stool Color |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Prehepatic** (hemolytic) | ↑↑↑ | Normal | Negative (unconjugated is albumin-bound, can't pass through kidney) | ↑↑ | Dark (↑stercobilin) |
| **Hepatic** (hepatocellular) | ↑ | ↑ | Positive | ↑ or normal | Normal or pale |
| **Posthepatic** (obstructive) | Normal or slight ↑ | ↑↑↑ | Positive (dark/tea-colored urine) | ↓ or absent | Clay/pale (no stercobilin reaching intestines) |

> **Exam pearl**: Conjugated bilirubin is water-soluble → can appear in urine. Unconjugated bilirubin is albumin-bound → NEVER appears in urine.

#### Liver Enzymes — Pattern Recognition

| Enzyme | Primary Source | Elevation Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| ALT (SGPT) | Liver (most specific for liver) | Hepatocellular damage; most specific liver enzyme |
| AST (SGOT) | Liver, heart, skeletal muscle | Hepatocellular damage; also elevated in MI and muscle injury |
| ALP (Alkaline Phosphatase) | Liver (bile duct), bone, placenta | Obstructive liver disease, bone disease (Paget's, bone mets); elevated in pregnancy (placental ALP) |
| GGT | Liver (bile duct), kidney | Obstructive liver disease, alcohol abuse; most sensitive for alcohol use. Helps differentiate liver vs. bone source of ↑ALP (GGT elevated in liver disease, normal in bone disease) |
| LD (LDH) | All tissues | Very nonspecific; LD isoenzymes: LD1 > LD2 = "flipped" pattern seen in MI and hemolytic anemia |

> **AST:ALT ratio**: In alcoholic liver disease, AST:ALT > 2:1 (classic 2:1 ratio). In viral hepatitis, ALT > AST.

### 3.4 Enzymes — Cardiac Markers

| Marker | Rise | Peak | Return to Normal | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Troponin I/T** | 3–6 hours | 12–24 hours | 7–14 days | MOST SPECIFIC for myocardial injury; preferred marker for MI diagnosis |
| **CK-MB** | 4–6 hours | 12–24 hours | 48–72 hours | More specific than total CK for heart; useful for detecting reinfarction |
| **Myoglobin** | 1–3 hours | 6–9 hours | 24 hours | EARLIEST marker, but NOT specific (found in all muscle) |
| **BNP/NT-proBNP** | N/A | N/A | N/A | Released by ventricles in response to volume/pressure overload; used for CHF diagnosis/monitoring, NOT MI |

**CK Isoenzymes**:
- CK-BB (CK-1): Brain — rarely measured
- CK-MB (CK-2): Heart — cardiac marker
- CK-MM (CK-3): Skeletal muscle — most abundant CK isoenzyme normally
- **Macro-CK**: CK-BB bound to immunoglobulin or CK-Mt (mitochondrial); causes persistent CK elevation without clinical cause

### 3.5 Proteins and Nitrogen Compounds

#### Serum Protein Electrophoresis (SPE) — Zones

| Zone (anode → cathode) | Major Proteins | Clinical Correlations |
|---|---|---|
| Albumin | Albumin | ↓ in liver disease, nephrotic syndrome, malnutrition, burns |
| Alpha-1 | α1-antitrypsin, α1-acid glycoprotein | ↓ α1-antitrypsin: emphysema, neonatal hepatitis |
| Alpha-2 | Haptoglobin, ceruloplasmin, α2-macroglobulin | ↓ haptoglobin: hemolysis. ↓ ceruloplasmin: Wilson disease |
| Beta | Transferrin, C3, β-lipoprotein | Beta-gamma bridging = cirrhosis |
| Gamma | Immunoglobulins (IgG, IgA, IgM, IgD, IgE) | Monoclonal spike (M-spike) = multiple myeloma or Waldenström's |

#### Renal Function Markers
- **BUN**: 6–20 mg/dL. ↑ in renal disease, dehydration, high-protein diet, GI bleeding, CHF
- **Creatinine**: 0.8–1.2 mg/dL. More specific for renal function than BUN (not affected by diet or hydration). Product of muscle creatine phosphate metabolism.
- **BUN:Creatinine ratio**: Normal = 10:1 to 20:1. **Prerenal** (dehydration, CHF): ratio > 20:1. **Renal** (intrinsic kidney disease): ratio 10:1 to 15:1. **Postrenal** (obstruction): ratio > 20:1.
- **Creatinine Clearance**: Estimates GFR. CrCl = (Urine Cr × Urine Volume) / (Plasma Cr × Time)
- **Cystatin C**: Alternative GFR marker; not affected by muscle mass, sex, or age like creatinine

### 3.6 Acid-Base, Blood Gases, Electrolytes

#### Acid-Base — THE TABLE YOU MUST MASTER

| Disorder | pH | pCO2 | HCO3- | Compensation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Metabolic Acidosis | ↓ | ↓ (compensatory) | ↓ (primary) | Respiratory: hyperventilation to blow off CO2 |
| Metabolic Alkalosis | ↑ | ↑ (compensatory) | ↑ (primary) | Respiratory: hypoventilation to retain CO2 |
| Respiratory Acidosis | ↓ | ↑ (primary) | ↑ (compensatory) | Metabolic: kidneys retain HCO3- |
| Respiratory Alkalosis | ↓ | ↓ (primary) | ↓ (compensatory) | Metabolic: kidneys excrete HCO3- |

**Reference ranges (arterial blood gas)**:
- pH: 7.35–7.45
- pCO2: 35–44 mmHg
- pO2: > 80 mmHg
- O2 saturation: > 95%
- HCO3-: 23–29 mmol/L

**How to read blood gases** (3-step approach):
1. Look at pH → acidosis (< 7.35) or alkalosis (> 7.45)?
2. Look at pCO2 → if it explains the pH, it's respiratory
3. Look at HCO3- → if it explains the pH, it's metabolic

#### Anion Gap
**Anion Gap = Na+ – (Cl- + HCO3-)**
- Normal: 8–16 mEq/L (with K+) or 10–12 mEq/L (without K+)
- **Increased anion gap metabolic acidosis** = **MUDPILES**: Methanol, Uremia, DKA, Propylene glycol, Isoniazid/Iron, Lactic acidosis, Ethylene glycol, Salicylates
- **Normal anion gap (hyperchloremic) metabolic acidosis**: Diarrhea, renal tubular acidosis, normal saline excess

- **Graves' disease**: Autoantibodies stimulate TSH receptor (thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulin, TSI)

#### Adrenal Function
- **Cushing syndrome** (↑cortisol): moon face, buffalo hump, hypertension, hyperglycemia, ↓eosinophils
- **Addison disease** (↓cortisol, ↓aldosterone): hypotension, hyperkalemia, hyponatremia, hyperpigmentation
- **Pheochromocytoma**: Adrenal medulla tumor producing catecholamines (epinephrine, norepinephrine) → episodic hypertension, headaches, sweating. Test: urine metanephrines, VMA

### 3.8 Therapeutic Drug Monitoring (TDM) & Toxicology

#### Key TDM Concepts
- **Trough level**: Drawn just BEFORE next dose (most important for avoiding toxicity)
- **Peak level**: Drawn after drug distribution is complete (for aminoglycosides: 30 min after IV infusion; for vancomycin: trough-only monitoring is now preferred)
- **Steady state**: Reached after 4–5 half-lives

#### Drugs to Know

| Drug | Category | Key Toxicity | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gentamicin/Tobramycin | Aminoglycosides | Nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity | Narrow therapeutic index |
| Vancomycin | Glycopeptide | Nephrotoxicity, "Red man syndrome" | Trough-based monitoring |
| Digoxin | Cardiac glycoside | Arrhythmias, visual changes | Hypokalemia potentiates toxicity |
| Phenytoin | Anticonvulsant | Gingival hyperplasia, nystagmus | Highly protein-bound; ↓ albumin → ↑ free drug |
| Lithium | Mood stabilizer | Nephrogenic diabetes insipidus, thyroid dysfunction | Narrow therapeutic range; hemolysis releases lithium from RBCs → falsely elevated |
| Tacrolimus/Cyclosporine | Immunosuppressants | Nephrotoxicity, neurotoxicity | Whole blood specimen required (drug distributes into RBCs) |

#### Toxicology

| Substance | Test | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ethanol | Enzymatic (ADH method) or GC | Legal limit 0.08 g/dL; osmol gap elevated |
| Methanol | GC (not enzymatic) | Metabolized to formic acid → blindness; ↑ osmol gap, ↑ anion gap |
| Ethylene glycol | GC | Metabolized to oxalic acid → calcium oxalate crystals in urine → renal failure |
| Acetaminophen | Immunoassay | Hepatotoxicity; antidote = N-acetylcysteine (NAC) |
| Salicylate (aspirin) | Trinder method (colorimetric) | Mixed respiratory alkalosis + metabolic acidosis |
| Lead | Atomic absorption or anodic stripping voltammetry | Basophilic stippling on peripheral smear; ↑ erythrocyte protoporphyrin (ZPP/FEP); inhibits ALA dehydratase and ferrochelatase |

**Scenario CHEM-1**: A 52-year-old obese male presents with polyuria, polydipsia, and blurred vision. Fasting glucose: 210 mg/dL. HbA1c: 9.2%.

**Q: Which of the following conditions would cause a FALSELY DECREASED HbA1c?**
A) Iron deficiency anemia
B) Splenectomy
C) Hemolytic anemia
D) Chronic renal failure

**Answer: C) Hemolytic anemia**
Rationale: Hemolytic anemia shortens RBC lifespan, so there is less time for glycation to occur → falsely low HbA1c. Iron deficiency anemia (A) and splenectomy (B) prolong RBC lifespan → falsely high HbA1c. Chronic renal failure (D) may also cause carbamylated hemoglobin interference depending on the method.

---

**Scenario CHEM-2**: A patient's arterial blood gas results show: pH 7.28, pCO2 55 mmHg, HCO3- 26 mmol/L.

**Q: What is this patient's acid-base status?**
A) Metabolic acidosis
B) Respiratory acidosis, uncompensated
C) Respiratory acidosis, partially compensated
D) Mixed metabolic and respiratory acidosis

**Answer: B) Respiratory acidosis, uncompensated**
Rationale: pH is low (acidosis). pCO2 is elevated (> 44) → respiratory cause. HCO3- is 26, which is within normal range (23–29), meaning the kidneys have NOT yet compensated. If partially compensated, HCO3- would be elevated above normal.

**Scenario CHEM-3**: A 45-year-old woman presents with fatigue, constipation, weight gain, and cold intolerance. Labs show: TSH = 15.2 mIU/L (ref: 0.4–4.0), Free T4 = 0.3 ng/dL (ref: 0.8–1.8).

**Q: These findings are most consistent with:**
A) Graves' disease
B) Primary hypothyroidism
C) Secondary hypothyroidism
D) Subclinical hypothyroidism

**Answer: B) Primary hypothyroidism**
Rationale: Elevated TSH + low Free T4 = primary hypothyroidism (the thyroid gland itself is failing, so the pituitary produces excess TSH trying to stimulate it). In secondary hypothyroidism (pituitary failure), TSH would be LOW. In Graves' disease, TSH is suppressed and T4 is elevated. Subclinical hypothyroidism has elevated TSH but normal T4.

## 4. HEMATOLOGY (17–22%)

### 4.1 Hematopoiesis and Normal Blood Cell Development

#### Maturation Sequence
- **Myeloid lineage**: Myeloblast → Promyelocyte → Myelocyte → Metamyelocyte → Band → Segmented neutrophil
  - Primary (azurophilic, nonspecific) granules appear at: **Promyelocyte** stage
  - Secondary (specific) granules appear at: **Myelocyte** stage
  - Cell stops dividing at: **Metamyelocyte** stage (post-mitotic)
- **Erythroid lineage**: Pronormoblast → Basophilic normoblast → Polychromatic normoblast → Orthochromic normoblast → Reticulocyte → Mature RBC
  - Nucleus extruded at: **Orthochromic normoblast** → reticulocyte stage
  - Hemoglobin synthesis begins at: **Basophilic normoblast**
- **Megakaryocyte lineage**: Megakaryoblast → Promegakaryocyte → Megakaryocyte → Platelets
  - Endomitosis (nuclear division without cytoplasmic division) → large, multilobed nucleus

### 4.2 Anemias — THE BIG TOPIC

#### Classification by MCV

**Microcytic (MCV < 76 fL) — "TICS"**:
- **T**halassemia
- **I**ron deficiency
- **C**hronic disease (anemia of inflammation)
- **S**ideroblastic anemia

**Normocytic (MCV 76–100 fL)**:
- Acute blood loss
- Hemolytic anemias (unless reticulocytosis raises MCV)
- Anemia of chronic disease (can also be microcytic)
- Aplastic anemia
- Chronic renal failure (↓ EPO)

**Macrocytic (MCV > 100 fL)**:
- **Megaloblastic**: B12 deficiency, folate deficiency — characterized by hypersegmented neutrophils (≥ 5 lobes), oval macrocytes
- **Non-megaloblastic**: Liver disease, alcoholism, reticulocytosis, myelodysplastic syndromes, hypothyroidism

#### Iron Deficiency Anemia — Most Common Anemia Worldwide
- Peripheral smear: Microcytic, hypochromic RBCs with anisocytosis and poikilocytosis; pencil cells (elliptocytes)
- Iron studies: ↓ serum iron, ↑ TIBC, ↓ ferritin, ↓ % saturation
- ↑ RDW (anisocytosis) — helps distinguish from thalassemia trait (which has normal RDW)
- Stages: Iron depletion → iron-deficient erythropoiesis → iron deficiency anemia

#### Thalassemia
- **Alpha-thalassemia**: Deletion of alpha-globin genes (chromosome 16)
  - 1 gene deleted: Silent carrier (normal)
  - 2 genes deleted: Alpha-thal trait (mild microcytic anemia)
  - 3 genes deleted: HbH disease (moderate hemolytic anemia; HbH = β4 tetramers; golf-ball inclusions with supravital stain)
  - 4 genes deleted: Hydrops fetalis (Hb Bart's = γ4; incompatible with life)
- **Beta-thalassemia**: Mutations of beta-globin genes (chromosome 11)
  - Beta-thal minor (trait): ↑ HbA2 (> 3.5%), mild microcytic anemia, target cells
  - Beta-thal major (Cooley anemia): severe anemia, transfusion-dependent, ↑ HbF, absent or severely reduced HbA
  - Beta-thal intermedia: moderate severity between minor and major

#### Hemoglobin Electrophoresis — MUST KNOW Migration Pattern
- At alkaline pH (cellulose acetate): Migration toward anode (+): **C → S → F → A** (mnemonic: "**C**areful **S**low **F**leas **A**head")
  - HbA: fastest migrating
  - HbC: slowest migrating
- At acid pH (citrate agar): Used to separate hemoglobins that co-migrate at alkaline pH
  - HbS and HbD migrate together at alkaline pH → acid pH separates them
  - HbC and HbE migrate together at alkaline pH → acid pH separates them

#### Sickle Cell Disease (HbSS)
- Valine substitutes for glutamic acid at position 6 of beta-globin chain
- Sickle cells (drepanocytes) on smear
- Sickle solubility test (dithionite tube test): Turbid = positive for HbS. Does NOT distinguish SS from AS (trait) — must use electrophoresis
- Newborn with sickle cell disease: HbF is predominant at birth → sickle solubility test may be NEGATIVE in neonates → use electrophoresis
- Target cells also present

#### Hemolytic Anemias — Important Features

| Type | Key Smear Finding | Key Lab Finding | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hereditary Spherocytosis | Spherocytes | ↑ MCHC, positive osmotic fragility, positive EMA binding test | Spectrin/ankyrin deficiency |
| G6PD Deficiency | Bite cells, Heinz bodies | Normal between episodes | X-linked; triggered by oxidant stress (drugs, fava beans, infection) |
| Sickle Cell | Sickle cells (drepanocytes) | Positive sickle solubility | HbSS; target cells also present |
| Autoimmune Hemolytic (WAIHA) | Spherocytes | Positive DAT (IgG ± C3d) | Often secondary to lymphoma, SLE, drugs |
| Cold Agglutinin Disease | RBC agglutinates at room temp | Positive DAT (C3d only); ↑ cold agglutinin titer | Associated with Mycoplasma, lymphoma |
| TTP | Schistocytes | Thrombocytopenia, ↑ LD, normal PT/PTT, ↓ ADAMTS13 | Classic pentad: microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia, renal failure, neurologic symptoms, fever |
| HUS | Schistocytes | Similar to TTP but predominantly renal failure | Often post-E. coli O157:H7 (Shiga toxin) |
| DIC | Schistocytes | ↑ PT, ↑ PTT, ↓ fibrinogen, ↑ D-dimer, ↓ platelets | Consumption of factors AND platelets |

> **Exam pearl**: If you see schistocytes (helmet cells, fragments) on a smear → think microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (MAHA): TTP, HUS, DIC, HELLP, mechanical heart valve

### 4.3 Leukocyte Disorders

#### Benign Leukocyte Disorders
- **Left shift**: Increased bands and immature granulocytes → bacterial infection
- **Reactive (atypical) lymphocytes**: Large, variable shape, blue cytoplasm molding around RBCs → EBV (infectious mono), CMV, hepatitis, toxoplasmosis
- **Döhle bodies**: Pale blue cytoplasmic inclusions in neutrophils → severe infection, pregnancy, burns
- **Toxic granulation**: Dark, prominent primary granules in neutrophils → severe infection/inflammation
- **Auer rods**: Red, needle-like cytoplasmic inclusions → ONLY in myeloid neoplasms (AML, especially APL)
- **Pelger-Huët anomaly**: Bilobed ("pince-nez" / sunglasses) neutrophils → congenital (true Pelger-Huët) or acquired (pseudo-Pelger-Huët in MDS)

#### Acute Leukemias — AML vs. ALL

| Feature | AML | ALL |
|---|---|---|
| Age | Adults (median ~65 years) | Children (peak 2–5 years) |
| Auer rods | Yes (pathognomonic) | No |
| MPO (Myeloperoxidase) | Positive | Negative |
| Sudan Black B | Positive | Negative |
| PAS | Negative (or diffusely positive) | Block-positive (coarse granules) |
| TdT | Negative (usually) | Positive |
| NSE (Nonspecific esterase) | Positive in monocytic (M4, M5) | Negative |
| CD markers | CD13, CD33, CD117, MPO | CD10 (CALLA), CD19, CD20 (B-ALL); CD2, CD3, CD5, CD7 (T-ALL) |

#### Key AML Subtypes

| WHO Classification | Key Features |
|---|---|
| AML with t(8;21) | Good prognosis; matures partially |
| APL with t(15;17) PML-RARA | Auer rods ("faggot cells" = multiple Auer rods), DIC is common; treated with ATRA (all-trans retinoic acid); medical EMERGENCY |
| AML with inv(16) | Abnormal eosinophils; good prognosis |
| AML with t(9;11) | Monocytic differentiation |

#### Chronic Leukemias

| Feature | CML | CLL |
|---|---|---|
| Cell type | Granulocytic (neutrophilic) | Mature B-lymphocytes |
| Hallmark | Philadelphia chromosome t(9;22) BCR-ABL1 | Smudge cells on smear |

#### Myeloproliferative Neoplasms — JAK2 Mutation

| Disease | Key Feature | JAK2 V617F | Other |
|---|---|---|---|
| Polycythemia Vera (PV) | ↑ RBC mass, ↑ Hgb/Hct | ~95% positive | ↓ EPO; ↑ all three cell lines |

### 4.4 Hemostasis

#### Coagulation Cascade — Simplified

**Extrinsic pathway** (tested by PT):
- Tissue Factor (TF) → Factor VII → Xa

**Intrinsic pathway** (tested by PTT/APTT):
- XII → XI → IX → (VIII as cofactor) → Xa

**Common pathway** (tested by BOTH PT and PTT):
- Xa → (V as cofactor) → Thrombin (IIa) → Fibrinogen (I) → Fibrin → Factor XIIIa crosslinks fibrin

#### Coagulation Tests Interpretation

| Test | Pathway | Factors Measured |
|---|---|---|
| PT/INR | Extrinsic + Common | VII, X, V, II, I (fibrinogen) |
| APTT | Intrinsic + Common | XII, XI, IX, VIII, X, V, II, I |
| Thrombin Time | Common (fibrinogen) | Fibrinogen quantity and quality; prolonged by heparin, fibrin degradation products, dysfibrinogenemia |

#### Mixing Study Interpretation
Mix patient plasma 1:1 with normal pooled plasma:
- **Corrects** → Factor deficiency (patient is missing a factor; the normal plasma supplies it)
- **Does NOT correct** → Inhibitor present (e.g., lupus anticoagulant, factor-specific inhibitor like Factor VIII inhibitor)
- **Lupus anticoagulant**: Prolongs PTT in vitro but causes THROMBOSIS in vivo (paradox!)

#### Common Bleeding Disorders

| Disorder | PT | PTT | Platelets | Key Features |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hemophilia A | Normal | ↑ | Normal | Factor VIII deficiency; X-linked |
| Hemophilia B | Normal | ↑ | Normal | Factor IX deficiency (Christmas disease); X-linked |
| von Willebrand Disease | Normal | ↑ or Normal | Normal or ↓ | Most common hereditary bleeding disorder; ↓ vWF → ↓ Factor VIII (vWF stabilizes VIII); ↑ bleeding time; ↓ ristocetin cofactor activity |
| DIC | ↑ | ↑ | ↓ | ↓ fibrinogen, ↑ D-dimer, schistocytes |
| Liver Disease | ↑ (first) | ↑ | ↓ or normal | Liver makes all factors except vWF and Factor VIII |
| Vitamin K Deficiency | ↑ (first) | ↑ | Normal | Factors II, VII, IX, X depend on vitamin K (mnemonic: "1972" — factors 10, 9, 7, 2) |
| Heparin therapy | Normal or slight ↑ | ↑↑ | Normal | Monitor with PTT (unfractionated) or anti-Xa (LMWH) |
| Warfarin therapy | ↑ | Normal or slight ↑ | Normal | Monitor with PT/INR; targets vitamin K-dependent factors |

### Hematology Clinical Scenarios

---

**Scenario HEM-1**: A 5-year-old child presents with pallor, fatigue, bone pain, and hepatosplenomegaly. CBC shows WBC 85 × 10³/μL, Hgb 6.2 g/dL, Plt 22 × 10³/μL. Peripheral smear shows numerous small blasts with scant cytoplasm and no granules. Flow cytometry shows CD10+, CD19+, CD20+, TdT+.

**Q: What is the most likely diagnosis?**
A) Acute myeloid leukemia
B) B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia
C) Chronic lymphocytic leukemia
D) Chronic myeloid leukemia in blast crisis

**Answer: B) B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL)**
Rationale: A child with blasts showing CD10 (CALLA), CD19, CD20 (B-cell markers), and TdT positivity is classic for B-ALL. ALL is the most common childhood malignancy. AML blasts would be MPO+, CD13+, CD33+. CLL occurs in older adults with mature lymphocytes. CML blast crisis is rare in children.

---

**Scenario HEM-2**: A 35-year-old woman of Southeast Asian descent has Hgb 10.2, MCV 62, MCH 22, MCHC 30, RDW 14.0% (normal). Iron studies are all normal. Hemoglobin electrophoresis shows HbA2 = 5.8% (ref: < 3.5%).

**Q: What is the most likely diagnosis?**
A) Iron deficiency anemia
B) Alpha-thalassemia trait
C) Beta-thalassemia minor (trait)
D) Sideroblastic anemia

**Answer: C) Beta-thalassemia minor**
Rationale: Microcytic anemia with NORMAL iron studies and NORMAL RDW (consistent cell size) + elevated HbA2 (> 3.5%) = beta-thalassemia trait. Iron deficiency would show abnormal iron studies and elevated RDW. Alpha-thal trait does NOT show elevated HbA2.

---

**Scenario HEM-3**: A 70-year-old male presents with a WBC of 125 × 10³/μL. Peripheral smear shows a monotonous population of small, mature-appearing lymphocytes with numerous smudge cells. Flow cytometry shows CD5+, CD19+, CD20 dim, CD23+.

**Q: What is the most likely diagnosis?**
A) Mantle cell lymphoma
B) Hairy cell leukemia
C) Chronic lymphocytic leukemia
D) Acute lymphoblastic leukemia

**Answer: C) Chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL)**
Rationale: Elderly patient + massive lymphocytosis + mature small lymphocytes + smudge cells + CD5+/CD19+/CD20(dim)/CD23+ = classic CLL. Mantle cell lymphoma is CD5+ but CD23-negative and has t(11;14). Hairy cell leukemia shows hairy projections on cells and is CD25+, CD103+, CD11c+. ALL has blasts, not mature cells.

---

**Scenario HEM-4**: A patient on warfarin has a PT of 22 seconds (reference 10–13 sec), INR 2.5, and a normal PTT. The PT corrects on a 1:1 mixing study with normal plasma.

**Q: What does the mixing study result indicate?**
A) Lupus anticoagulant
B) Factor VIII inhibitor
C) Factor VII deficiency
D) Factor deficiency corrected by normal plasma

**Answer: D) Factor deficiency corrected by normal plasma**
Rationale: A prolonged PT that CORRECTS with mixing indicates a factor deficiency, not an inhibitor. This patient is on warfarin, which depletes vitamin K-dependent factors (II, VII, IX, X). The normal plasma provides the missing factors. An inhibitor would NOT correct. Factor VII is the most likely specific deficiency since only PT (not PTT) is prolonged, but D is the best general answer.

### Microbiology Clinical Scenarios

---

**Scenario MICRO-1**: A sputum culture from a 60-year-old patient with pneumonia grows mucoid, lactose-positive colonies on MacConkey agar. Gram stain shows large gram-negative rods with a prominent capsule. The organism is non-motile, indole-negative, and urease-positive.

**Q: What is the most likely organism?**
A) Escherichia coli
B) Klebsiella pneumoniae
C) Pseudomonas aeruginosa
D) Enterobacter aerogenes

**Answer: B) Klebsiella pneumoniae**
Rationale: Mucoid lactose-positive colonies + encapsulated large GNR + non-motile + indole-negative = Klebsiella. E. coli is lactose+ but indole+ and not typically mucoid. Pseudomonas is a non-fermenter (non-lactose-fermenting). Enterobacter is motile.

---

**Scenario MICRO-2**: A blood culture from a 28-year-old IV drug user grows gram-positive cocci in clusters. The organism is catalase-positive, coagulase-positive, beta-hemolytic, and ferments mannitol on mannitol salt agar.

**Q: What is the organism, and what test would confirm MRSA?**
A) Staph epidermidis; Novobiocin susceptibility
B) Staph aureus; Cefoxitin disk screen or mecA PCR
C) Strep pyogenes; Bacitracin susceptibility
D) Enterococcus faecalis; Bile esculin test

**Answer: B) Staph aureus; Cefoxitin disk screen or mecA PCR**
Rationale: GPC in clusters + catalase+ + coagulase+ + beta-hemolytic + mannitol fermenter = S. aureus. To determine if it's MRSA, use a cefoxitin disk screen (zone ≤ 21 mm = resistant = MRSA) or molecular detection of mecA gene.

---

**Scenario MICRO-3**: A stool specimen from a 3-year-old child with bloody diarrhea is plated on MacConkey and sorbitol-MacConkey agar. Colorless (non-lactose-fermenting) colonies grow on MacConkey. On sorbitol-MacConkey, the colonies are also colorless (non-sorbitol-fermenting).

**Q: Which organism should be suspected, and what serious complication can it cause?**
A) Shigella sonnei; Guillain-Barré syndrome
B) Campylobacter jejuni; Reactive arthritis
C) E. coli O157:H7; Hemolytic uremic syndrome (HUS)
D) Salmonella enteritidis; Osteomyelitis

**Answer: C) E. coli O157:H7; Hemolytic uremic syndrome (HUS)**
Rationale: E. coli O157:H7 does NOT ferment sorbitol (unlike most other E. coli), producing colorless colonies on SMAC. It produces Shiga-like toxin (verotoxin) that can lead to HUS, especially in children. HUS = triad of microangiopathic hemolytic anemia + thrombocytopenia + acute renal failure.

### Urinalysis/Body Fluids Clinical Scenarios

---

**Scenario UA-1**: A 25-year-old male presents with dark-colored urine, periorbital edema, and hypertension 2 weeks after a strep throat infection. UA shows: Color = dark brown/cola, Blood = 3+, Protein = 2+, RBCs = too numerous to count, RBC casts = present.

C) Nephrotic syndrome
D) Acute tubular necrosis

## 7. IMMUNOLOGY (5–10%)

### 7.1 Immune System Fundamentals

#### Immunoglobulins

| Class | Properties | Clinical Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **IgG** | Most abundant (75–80%); crosses placenta; secondary immune response; 4 subclasses | Only Ig that crosses placenta → protects newborn; opsonization; complement activation (classical) |
| **IgM** | Largest (pentamer); first antibody in primary immune response; does NOT cross placenta | Best complement activator; isohemagglutinins (anti-A, anti-B); does not cross placenta → if IgM found in neonate = active fetal infection (TORCH) |
| **IgA** | Dimer in secretions (sIgA); protects mucosal surfaces (GI, respiratory, GU tracts) | Secretory piece added by epithelial cells; predominant Ig in saliva, tears, breast milk, GI secretions |
| **IgE** | Monomer; lowest serum concentration | Binds mast cells and basophils; type I hypersensitivity (allergic reactions, anaphylaxis); elevated in parasitic infections and atopy |
| **IgD** | Monomer; found on B-cell surface | B-cell receptor; function not fully understood |

> **Memory for Ig concentrations (highest to lowest)**: G-A-M-D-E ("**GAMDE**")

#### Complement System
- **Classical pathway**: Activated by antigen-antibody complexes (IgG, IgM) → C1q → C1r → C1s → C4 → C2 → C3
- **Alternative pathway**: Activated by microbial surfaces directly (no antibody needed) → Factor B, Factor D, Properdin → C3
- **Lectin pathway**: Mannose-binding lectin (MBL) binds mannose on microbial surfaces → activates C4, C2 → C3
- **All pathways converge at C3** → C5 → C6, C7, C8, C9 = **Membrane Attack Complex (MAC)**
- **C3** is the most abundant complement component
- **C3a, C5a**: Anaphylatoxins (cause inflammation, mast cell degranulation); C5a is also a potent chemotactic factor
- **C3b**: Opsonin (promotes phagocytosis)
- **CH50**: Screens the classical pathway (all components C1–C9 must be present); 0 CH50 = complete deficiency of one component

#### Hypersensitivity Reactions

| Type | Name | Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Immediate (Anaphylactic) | IgE + mast cells/basophils → histamine release | Allergies, anaphylaxis, asthma, hay fever |
| II | Cytotoxic (Antibody-mediated) | IgG or IgM against cell surface antigens | Hemolytic transfusion reactions, HDFN, autoimmune hemolytic anemia, Goodpasture syndrome, Graves' disease, myasthenia gravis |
| III | Immune complex | Antigen-antibody complexes deposit in tissues | Serum sickness, SLE, post-strep glomerulonephritis, Arthus reaction |
| IV | Delayed-type (Cell-mediated) | T cells (no antibody) | TB skin test (PPD/Mantoux), contact dermatitis, transplant rejection (chronic), granulomas |

### Immunology Clinical Scenarios

---

**Scenario IMM-1**: A 35-year-old woman presents with joint pain, butterfly (malar) rash on her face, and fatigue. ANA is positive at 1:640 with a homogeneous pattern. Anti-dsDNA is positive. C3 and C4 complement levels are decreased.

## 8. LABORATORY OPERATIONS (5–10%)

### 8.1 Quality Control (QC) and Quality Assurance (QA)

#### Levey-Jennings Chart and Westgard Rules

A Levey-Jennings chart plots QC results over time. The mean ± 1SD, 2SD, and 3SD lines are drawn. Westgard rules determine when a QC run should be rejected:

| Rule | What It Means | Type of Error |
|---|---|---|
| **1-2s** | 1 control exceeds ±2 SD | WARNING only — do NOT reject; investigate |
| **1-3s** | 1 control exceeds ±3 SD | REJECT — random error |
| **2-2s** | 2 consecutive controls exceed the same ±2 SD | REJECT — systematic error (shift) |
| **R-4s** | Range between 2 controls within a run exceeds 4 SD | REJECT — random error |
| **4-1s** | 4 consecutive controls on the same side of the mean, all exceed ±1 SD | REJECT — systematic error (shift or trend) |
| **10-x** | 10 consecutive controls on the same side of the mean | REJECT — systematic error (shift) |

> **Shift**: Sudden change in QC level (all points above or below mean) → systematic error → recalibrate
> **Trend**: Gradual, progressive change in QC results in one direction → systematic error → deteriorating reagent, light source

#### Accuracy vs. Precision
- **Accuracy**: How close results are to the TRUE VALUE (measured by proficiency testing)
- **Precision**: How REPRODUCIBLE results are (measured by repeated QC testing)
- You can be precise but not accurate (systematic error) or accurate but not precise (random error)

#### Sensitivity, Specificity, Predictive Value

|  | Disease Present | Disease Absent |
|---|---|---|
| Test Positive | True Positive (TP) | False Positive (FP) |
| Test Negative | False Negative (FN) | True Negative (TN) |

- **Sensitivity** = TP / (TP + FN) × 100 = ability to detect disease (rule OUT) — "SnNout" (high sensitivity, negative result rules out)
- **Specificity** = TN / (TN + FP) × 100 = ability to exclude disease (rule IN) — "SpPin" (high specificity, positive result rules in)
- **PPV** = TP / (TP + FP) × 100 = probability that a positive result = true disease
- **NPV** = TN / (TN + FN) × 100 = probability that a negative result = truly no disease
- **Predictive values are affected by disease prevalence**; sensitivity and specificity are NOT

### 8.2 Laboratory Safety

- **Biosafety Level 1 (BSL-1)**: Agents not known to cause disease in healthy adults (e.g., E. coli K-12)
- **BSL-2**: Agents of moderate potential hazard; standard clinical lab work (most clinical specimens); biological safety cabinet (BSC) used
- **BSL-3**: Agents that cause serious/lethal disease via inhalation (M. tuberculosis, Brucella, Francisella, Coccidioides, SARS-CoV-2); BSC required, restricted access, respiratory protection
- **BSL-4**: Dangerous/exotic agents with high lethality and no vaccine/treatment (Ebola, Marburg); positive-pressure suits

**Standard Precautions**: Treat ALL blood and body fluids as potentially infectious (universal precautions)

**Safety Data Sheets (SDS)**: Formerly called MSDS. 16 standardized sections. Must be available for ALL chemicals in the lab. Required by OSHA.

**Fire extinguisher types**:
- Class A: Ordinary combustibles (paper, wood)
- Class B: Flammable liquids (grease, oil, gas)
- Class C: Electrical fires
- Class ABC: Multi-purpose (most common in labs)

### 8.3 Methodology and Instrumentation

| Method | Principle | Applications |
|---|---|---|
| Spectrophotometry | Beer's Law: A = εbc (absorbance = molar absorptivity × path length × concentration) | Most chemistry analyzers; measures color intensity |
| Nephelometry | Measures light scattered by particles at an angle | Immunoglobulin quantitation, CRP, complement |
| Turbidimetry | Measures decrease in transmitted light (absorption by particles) | Similar to nephelometry |
| Flow Cytometry | Laser excites fluorochrome-labeled cells → scatter and fluorescence measured | Cell surface markers, DNA content, immunophenotyping |
| Electrophoresis | Separation by charge and size in electric field | Serum protein electrophoresis, hemoglobin electrophoresis |
| Ion-selective electrode (ISE) | Selective membrane responds to specific ion | Na+, K+, Cl-, Ca2+, pH |
| HPLC | Separation by differential interaction with stationary phase | HbA1c, drugs, hemoglobin variants |
| Mass spectrometry (MALDI-TOF) | Ionize molecules → separate by mass-to-charge ratio | Microbial identification; drug testing (LC-MS/MS) |
| PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction) | Amplify DNA: Denaturation (94°C) → Annealing (50–65°C) → Extension (72°C) | Molecular diagnostics; pathogen detection; genetic testing |

#### Beer's Law: A = εbc
- A = Absorbance
- ε = Molar absorptivity (constant for a substance at a given wavelength)
- b = Path length (typically 1 cm)
- c = Concentration
- **Absorbance is directly proportional to concentration** (when Beer's Law is followed)
- Deviations from Beer's Law: stray light, fluorescence, concentration too high

### 8.4 Laboratory Mathematics

#### Dilutions
- **Dilution factor** = Volume of specimen / Total volume
- Example: 0.1 mL serum + 0.9 mL diluent = 0.1/1.0 = 1:10 dilution
- **Serial dilutions**: Multiply dilution factors. Example: 1:2 × 1:2 × 1:2 = 1:8
- **To calculate original concentration**: Measured value × reciprocal of dilution factor
  - Example: Measured = 50, dilution = 1:10, Original = 50 × 10 = 500

#### Concentration and Volume
- **C1V1 = C2V2** (dilution formula)
- Example: Need 100 mL of 0.5N NaOH from 10N stock: 10 × V1 = 0.5 × 100 → V1 = 5 mL

### Laboratory Operations Clinical Scenarios

---

**Scenario LAB-1**: On a Levey-Jennings chart, the last 12 QC values for glucose on the normal level control have been: 95, 97, 98, 99, 100, 101, 102, 103, 104, 105, 106, 107 mg/dL. The mean is 100 mg/dL and 1 SD = 3.

**Q: Which Westgard rule violation is demonstrated?**
A) 1-3s
B) R-4s
C) 10-x (shift)
D) 2-2s

**Answer: C) 10-x (shift)**
Rationale: The last 10+ values are all on the SAME SIDE of the mean (all above or progressively above the mean). This represents a systematic error — a trend or shift. This could indicate a deteriorating calibrator, aging reagent, or light source degradation. The 1-3s rule requires a single value > 3SD. R-4s requires a range > 4SD between two controls in one run.

---

**Scenario LAB-2**: A laboratory test has a sensitivity of 95% and specificity of 90%. The disease prevalence in the tested population is 1%.

**Q: If a patient tests positive, what is the approximate positive predictive value?**
A) 95%
B) 90%
C) ~9% (approximately 1 in 11)
D) 50%

**Answer: C) ~9%**
Rationale: With LOW disease prevalence, even a specific test will have many false positives relative to true positives. In a population of 10,000 with 1% prevalence: 100 have disease, 9,900 don't. TP = 95 (95% of 100), FP = 990 (10% of 9,900). PPV = 95/(95+990) = 95/1085 ≈ 8.8%. This illustrates why predictive values depend heavily on prevalence.

## 9. MUST-KNOW CALCULATIONS & FORMULAS

### Chemistry Calculations
1. **% Transferrin Saturation** = (Serum Iron / TIBC) × 100
2. **UIBC** = TIBC – Serum Iron
3. **Unconjugated (indirect) Bilirubin** = Total Bilirubin – Direct (Conjugated) Bilirubin
4. **LDL (Friedewald)** = Total Cholesterol – HDL – (Triglycerides / 5) — only valid if TG < 400
5. **Non-HDL Cholesterol** = Total Cholesterol – HDL
6. **A/G Ratio** = Albumin / (Total Protein – Albumin)
7. **Osmolality (calculated)** = 2(Na) + Glucose/18 + BUN/2.8
8. **Anion Gap** = Na – (Cl + HCO3)
9. **Corrected Calcium** = Measured Ca + 0.8 × (4.0 – Albumin)
10. **Creatinine Clearance** = (UCr × Volume) / (PCr × Time in minutes)
11. **Beer's Law**: A = εbc

### Hematology Calculations
1. **MCV** = (Hct × 10) / RBC count (in millions) — expressed in fL
2. **MCH** = (Hgb × 10) / RBC count (in millions) — expressed in pg
3. **MCHC** = (Hgb / Hct) × 100 — expressed in g/dL (or Hgb × 100 / Hct%)
4. **Corrected WBC** (when nRBCs present) = (WBC count × 100) / (100 + nRBCs per 100 WBCs)
5. **Absolute cell count** = WBC × (% cell type / 100)
   - Example: WBC = 8.0 × 10³/μL, 60% neutrophils → Absolute neutrophils = 8.0 × 0.60 = 4.8 × 10³/μL
6. **Reticulocyte count (absolute)** = WBC... no: **Absolute Retic** = Retic% × RBC count
7. **Corrected Retic count** = (Retic% × Patient Hct) / Normal Hct (45%)
8. **Reticulocyte Production Index (RPI)** = Corrected Retic Count / Maturation time
   - Maturation times: Hct 45% = 1 day; 35% = 1.5; 25% = 2; 15% = 2.5

### Blood Banking Calculations
1. **RhIG dose** = (% fetal cells from KB × maternal blood volume [5000 mL]) / 30 mL → round up + 1 vial

### Lab Math
1. **C1V1 = C2V2** (dilution preparation)
2. **Dilution factor** = sample volume / total volume
3. **Mean** = sum of values / number of values
4. **Standard Deviation**: Most instruments calculate this; know that 68% of values fall within ±1 SD, 95% within ±2 SD, 99.7% within ±3 SD
5. **Coefficient of Variation (CV%)** = (SD / Mean) × 100 — measures precision as a percentage

## FINAL STUDY TIPS

1. **Focus on the "Big Four"**: Blood Banking, Chemistry, Hematology, and Microbiology each make up 17–22% of the exam. Getting strong in these four areas covers ~70–88% of the exam.

2. **Memorize the reference ranges**: They will NOT be given on the exam. Make flashcards.

3. **Practice antibody panels**: Being able to rule out antigens and identify antibodies is one of the most frequently tested skills.

4. **Know your stains**: Gram stain reactions, acid-fast, special stains for hematology — these are high-yield recall questions.

5. **Understand disease correlations**: The ASCP doesn't just test facts — it tests your ability to correlate lab results with clinical conditions.

6. **Time management on CAT**: You have 2.5 hours for 100 questions = 1.5 minutes per question. Don't spend too long on any single question. The CAT algorithm gives you harder questions when you're doing well — if questions suddenly get harder, that's a GOOD sign.

7. **For Filipino MedTech graduates specifically**: Your curriculum covered these topics. The biggest gaps are usually in US-specific regulations (CLIA, AABB, FDA), blood banking antibody panels, and some newer molecular methods (MALDI-TOF, multiplex PCR). Focus extra time there.

8. **Take practice exams under timed conditions**: Simulate the real exam environment. Review every wrong answer thoroughly.
